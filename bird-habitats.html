Wetlands as Essential Habitats for Birds

Coastal wetlands and mangrove ecosystems provide important habitats for a wide variety of resident and migratory birds. Shallow water, intertidal mudflats, open wetland areas and mangrove vegetation provide feeding grounds, resting sites and shelter. These habitats are particularly important for migratory waterbirds that depend on suitable stopover and wintering sites along their migration routes.

The Thane Creek landscape supports diverse birdlife, including flamingos, shorebirds and other waterbirds. The availability of suitable feeding areas, water conditions, tidal patterns and habitat connectivity can influence how birds use different wetland sites.

Understanding bird habitats requires consideration of both ecological conditions and the wider landscape. Historical bird records published surveys and habitat information can help identify important locations and seasonal patterns. This section brings together available bird information to illustrate the ecological importance of wetlands and the need to conserve habitats that sustain migratory and resident bird populations.

Understanding Wetland Change Over Time

Wetlands are dynamic ecosystems that change in response to natural processes, seasonal variations and human activities. In rapidly urbanising landscapes such as Thane Creek, changes in land use, infrastructure development, reclamation and alterations in water flow can influence wetland extent, vegetation cover and habitat conditions.

LAND-USE / LAND-COVER

land-use/land-cover (LULC) classes

land-use/land-cover (LULC) classes

Water

Rivers, creeks, ponds and other areas covered by water.

Mangroves

Coastal vegetation that supports biodiversity, stores carbon and protects shorelines.

Flooded Vegetation

Areas where vegetation grows in waterlogged or seasonally inundated conditions.

Rangeland

Areas dominated by grasses, shrubs and other low vegetation.

Built Area

Land occupied by buildings, roads and other human-made infrastructure.

Bare Ground

Exposed soil, sand or land with little or no vegetation cover.

Land-use and land-cover (LULC) classification helps identify the distribution of different surface features across the Thane Creek landscape. The six classes—Water, Mangroves, Flooded Vegetation, Rangeland, Built Area and Bare Ground—represent distinct landscape characteristics. Comparing their distribution across different years can help reveal changes in vegetation cover, urban development, water distribution and the extent of exposed land.

Monitoring these changes over time helps identify areas of wetland persistence, expansion or potential loss. Satellite remote sensing and Geographic Information Systems (GIS) provide valuable tools for comparing land use and land cover across different years, identifying spatial patterns and understanding the changing relationship between wetlands and surrounding urban areas.

This section focuses on comparing wetland conditions across selected time periods to highlight landscape transformations and provide information that can support wetland management, ecological assessment and conservation planning.

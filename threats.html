Pressures on Wetland Ecosystems

Wetlands in rapidly developing urban regions face multiple environmental pressures. Urban expansion, land reclamation, infrastructure development, waste disposal, water pollution and changes in natural hydrological processes can alter wetland structure and ecological functioning. These pressures may reduce habitat availability, affect vegetation and disturb areas used by migratory birds.

Mangroves and intertidal habitats are especially important because they support biodiversity, contribute to coastal protection, store carbon and help maintain ecosystem functions. Changes in tidal exchange, sediment conditions or surrounding land use can influence the quality and continuity of these habitats.

Identifying environmental threats is an important step towards effective wetland conservation. Satellite imagery, published research, available environmental records and documented site observations can help reveal potential pressures and areas requiring further investigation. This section highlights key categories of environmental threats to encourage awareness, evidence-based assessment and informed conservation action.
